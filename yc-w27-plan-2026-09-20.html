Poplur beta → YC Winter 2027: the plan

Decided 20 September 2026, updated 21 September (no cash prizes; recognition instead). Day 0 is Thursday 1 October. Submit Tuesday 27 October. Hard deadline Monday 2 November, 8:00 p.m. Pacific (11:00 p.m. Toronto).

The shape of it. The invite goes out Thu 24 Sep. Students join the Discord, sign the NDA, get the Tester role and install before day 0, Thu 1 Oct. The first two weeks, 1–14 Oct, are the intensive: daily prompt, a fix build every two or three days, three short reports. Thu 15 Oct is the day-14 pull that feeds the application. The shoot week is 12–16 Oct, mid-beta, with the college's camera equipment. From 15 Oct the beta drops to a low-touch tail with the points board still open to Fri 31 Oct. The form is rewritten 15–21 Oct and submitted Tue 27 Oct. The beta keeps running through the 11 December decision. Both platforms from day 0: iPhone via TestFlight, Android 8.0+ via Google Play internal testing.

Two weeks and a month are not alternatives. The application only ever uses the first two weeks, because two weeks is the minimum that yields a day-14 retention number. The month is for the interview, where the first question is "what happened since you applied", and for the numbers on decision day.

What YC needs

Partners spend about ten minutes per application and look for four things: you launched to real people, you have a retention read, you shipped fixes fast while they used it, and you explain it clearly on video. A two-week opt-in student beta produces all four.

Form fields that carry the beta

  • Are people using your product? Today: No. Flipping it to Yes is the single change a partner reads most, and it must be backed by numbers in How far along.
  • How far along are you? Rewritten with the day-14 pull.
  • When will you have a version people can use? Already says "this semester", so 1 Oct fits without edits now.
  • Do you have revenue? No. A campus café posting a real business pop is the revenue story on screen, if you can get one.
  • Founder video, required. YC profile, required. Demo, optional.

Numbers to pull, humans only

  • Installs and sign-ups.
  • Posted a pop within 24 h of first open, as count / n.
  • D7 and D14: opened plus any action, as count / eligible.
  • Pops per active user; engaged pops that grew; re-post within 72 h.
  • Discovery by radius: feed/map interception vs profile visit.
  • Referral installs and off-prompt posts.
  • One reach story, one learning that changed the product, three verbatims.

Never write in the application: the IP rationale for having no users; any synthetic-cohort number where it could read as human traction, the 8-actor cohort is only the pre-launch simulation harness and gets its own sentence; any mention of teaching online or part-time during the batch; revenue estimates borrowed from another company's user count. Never write "validated"; write the numbers, n, dates and the one insight.

What to present to YC at the end of the beta

A one-page sheet for 15–27 October. YC scores founders and evidence, not ideas. A partner spends about ten minutes on the form and, if it goes well, ten minutes on a video interview in November. By 31 October you need to answer four questions with numbers you can defend.

The four questions

  • Did real people use it, and did they come back? For consumer social this is the whole ballgame. Retention beats installs, beats posts, beats everything. Day 7 and day 14 return, with honest denominators.
  • Does the core loop work between humans? Post, seen inside the radius, a classmate engages, the radius grows, the poster posts again. If that loop fired without you in it, the product exists. If it only fired with synthetic actors, it does not.
  • How fast do you ship and learn? YC reads iteration speed as the strongest founder signal. A fix build every two or three days, and at least one product change the data forced.
  • Can you explain it in a minute and defend it in ten? The founder video and the interview. Clarity and honesty about small numbers beat polish.

What the beta can prove, and how to report it

Fifteen to twenty students over two weeks cannot prove product-market fit, growth or revenue. Do not claim them. Counts with denominators and dates, never a bare percentage: "9 of 14 posted within a day" is credible, "64% activation" from 14 people is not.

  • Why this idea. One student quote, first name with permission, about why they opened it.

  • Demo, 3 min, optional but strongly recommended. Real student pops, a like growing a radius on a second phone, the best reach story.
  • Links. www.poplur.ca and the TestFlight link, both checked from a phone with no account.

Interview readiness, November

  • First question, every time: what happened since you applied? The tail numbers, 15–31 Oct, and retention after the daily prompts stopped are the answer.
  • Know every number cold: installs, day-14 return, the loop count, the reach story. No looking anything up.
  • One line each for: the biggest risk, why solo, why now and not 2017, what the $500K buys in the first city.
  • Keep the beta running. Numbers on 11 Dec count as much as numbers on 27 Oct.

The deal, for reference

Every accepted company gets $500,000: $125,000 for 7% on a post-money SAFE, plus $375,000 on an uncapped SAFE with a most-favoured-nation clause. No milestones, no fees, committed on acceptance. YC keeps pro rata rights. At 100% today you would hold 93% after YC.

The story a partner should walk away with, in five sentences. I applied in 2017 with a prototype and no users. I spent nine years teaching the people social media fails, and built the product. In October I launched to my students, opt-in, on both platforms. The radius loop fired between real people, [N] came back on day 14, and this is the one thing they taught me that changed the product. I am applying again because it is real now, and the next three months should be about the first city.
